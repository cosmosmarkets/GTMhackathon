Why most AI email doesn't sound like you

General-purpose AI writes toward an average. It has read billions of sentences and predicts the most likely next word — which means it defaults to safe, even, faintly corporate phrasing. The result is grammatically perfect and completely anonymous. Your colleague can tell a robot wrote it before they finish the first line.

The fix isn't a better prompt. It's a tool that starts from your writing, not everyone's.

What "sounds like me" actually means

Voice isn't vocabulary — it's pattern. Short punchy sentences vs. long flowing ones. Whether you think in parentheses or in lists. Whether you open warm or get straight to the ask. A tool can only protect your voice if it can read it first. That's the part most AI skips.

FAQ

How do you write AI email that still sounds like you?

Use an AI email tool that learns your voice from your own past writing instead of generating from a generic model. Lightfern reads how you actually write — your rhythm, phrasing, and signature moves — and drafts in that voice.

Why doesn't ChatGPT email sound like me?

General models write toward an average of all text, so they default to safe, even phrasing with no model of your specific voice. The output is competent but anonymous. Conditioning on your own writing first — what Lightfern does — is what fixes it.

Can AI really copy my writing style?

It's less "copy" and more "continue." Given enough of your real writing, a voice-aware tool can match your patterns closely enough that readers don't notice a tool was involved. The more samples it sees, the truer it gets.
